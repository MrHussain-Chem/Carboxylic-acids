CARBOXYLIC ACIDS AND DERIVATIVES - AQA A-LEVEL CHEMISTRY REVISION

Carboxylic Acid: R-COOH (contains carboxyl group)
Acid Derivatives: Compounds containing the acyl group (R-CO-)
Acylation: The process of introducing an acyl group into a molecule

1. CARBOXYLIC ACIDS PROPERTIES

Physical Properties

Property Explanation Example
High boiling points Form hydrogen bond DIMERS Ethanoic acid BP: 118°C vs Propan-1-ol: 97°C
Solubility Low Mr acids soluble (H-bonding) Methanoic to butanoic acids are water-soluble
Acidity WEAK acids (only partially ionise) RCOOH ⇌ RCOO⁻ + H⁺

Common mistake: Students often say carboxylic acids have high BP due to "hydrogen bonding". You MUST specify they form DIMERS (two molecules H-bonded together) - this is why their BP is higher than alcohols!

Acid Reactions

Key reactions to remember:
  • With bases: RCOOH + NaOH → RCOO⁻Na⁺ + H₂O (salt formation)
  • With carbonates: 2RCOOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2RCOO⁻Na⁺ + H₂O + CO₂
  • With metals: 2RCOOH + Mg → (RCOO⁻)₂Mg²⁺ + H₂
Test for carboxylic acids: Add Na₂CO₃ - effervescence indicates acid present

2. ACID DERIVATIVES - REACTIVITY ORDER

Reactivity Order (Most → Least reactive):
Acyl chloride > Acid anhydride > Ester > Amide

Key reason: Stability of leaving group + electron donation

Why This Order?

Derivative Formula Why reactive/unreactive?
Acyl chloride RCOCl Cl is electronegative (withdraws e⁻) + good leaving group
Acid anhydride (RCO)₂O RCOO⁻ is reasonable leaving group
Ester RCOOR' RO⁻ is poor leaving group
Amide RCONH₂ N donates e⁻ by resonance + NH₂⁻ terrible leaving group

3. PREPARATION OF ESTERS - THREE METHODS

Method 1: Carboxylic Acid + Alcohol (EQUILIBRIUM)

  • Equation: RCOOH + R'OH ⇌ RCOOR' + H₂O
  • Catalyst: Conc. H₂SO₄
  • Conditions: Heat under reflux
  • REVERSIBLE - low yield unless water removed!

Method 2: Acyl Chloride + Alcohol (GOES TO COMPLETION)

  • Equation: RCOCl + R'OH → RCOOR' + HCl
  • Conditions: Room temperature
  • Vigorous reaction - white fumes of HCl

Method 3: Acid Anhydride + Alcohol

  • Equation: (RCO)₂O + R'OH → RCOOR' + RCOOH
  • Conditions: Gentle warming
  • Used for ASPIRIN manufacture (safer than acyl chloride)

4. HYDROLYSIS OF ESTERS

Type Reagent Products Reversible?
Acid hydrolysis Dilute H₂SO₄/HCl + heat Carboxylic acid + alcohol YES ⇌
Alkaline hydrolysis NaOH(aq) + heat Carboxylate salt + alcohol NO →

Examiner's favourite: "Why is alkaline hydrolysis irreversible?" Answer: The carboxylic acid formed immediately reacts with excess OH⁻ to form the carboxylate salt, preventing the reverse reaction.

5. ACYL CHLORIDES - NUCLEOPHILIC ADDITION-ELIMINATION

All acyl chloride reactions follow the same mechanism:
1. Addition: Nucleophile attacks δ+ carbon
2. Elimination: Cl⁻ leaves, reforming C=O

Key Reactions of Acyl Chlorides

Nucleophile Product Other Product Observations
H₂O Carboxylic acid HCl White fumes, vigorous
ROH Ester HCl White fumes, vigorous
NH₃ Primary amide NH₄Cl White smoke, violent
RNH₂ Secondary amide RNH₃⁺Cl⁻ White solid, violent

With ammonia/amines, the HCl produced immediately reacts with excess base to form a salt. This is why we get NH₄Cl not HCl!

Mechanism Drawing Rules

Step 1 - Addition:
• Curly arrow FROM lone pair on nucleophile TO C=O carbon
• Another arrow FROM C=O bond TO oxygen
• Forms tetrahedral intermediate with O⁻

Step 2 - Elimination:
• Arrow FROM O⁻ TO reform C=O
• Arrow FROM C-Cl bond TO Cl (leaves as Cl⁻)
MUST show all charges and lone pairs!

6. ASPIRIN SYNTHESIS

Industrial Synthesis:

Salicylic acid + Ethanoic anhydride → Aspirin + Ethanoic acid

Why use anhydride not acyl chloride?

  • Cheaper to produce
  • Less corrosive (produces CH₃COOH not HCl)
  • Easier to control (less vigorous)
  • Safer - doesn't produce toxic HCl fumes

7. FATS, OILS AND BIODIESEL

Triglycerides:

  • Triesters of glycerol with fatty acids
  • Fats: Solid at room temp (saturated fatty acids)
  • Oils: Liquid at room temp (unsaturated fatty acids)

Biodiesel Production (Transesterification):

Triglyceride + 3CH₃OH → Biodiesel (methyl esters) + Glycerol

Catalyst: KOH

The glycerol by-product can be sold - makes process economical!

8. QUICK EXAM CHECKLIST

Before answering carboxylic acid questions:

  • ☐ Carboxylic acids form H-bond DIMERS (high BP)
  • ☐ They are WEAK acids (partially ionise)
  • ☐ Esterification with alcohols is REVERSIBLE
  • ☐ Alkaline hydrolysis is IRREVERSIBLE
  • ☐ Acyl chlorides most reactive (Cl withdraws electrons)
  • ☐ All acyl chloride reactions are VIGOROUS at RT
  • ☐ Mechanism = nucleophilic addition-elimination
  • ☐ Aspirin uses anhydride (safer than acyl chloride)
  • ☐ With NH₃ → amide + NH₄Cl (NOT HCl)

Common Exam Question:

Q: Suggest why ethanoic anhydride is preferred over ethanoyl chloride in aspirin manufacture.

Model Answer: Ethanoic anhydride is cheaper, less corrosive (produces ethanoic acid not HCl), the reaction is easier to control as it's less vigorous, and it's safer as it doesn't produce toxic HCl fumes.

Top Mark-Losing Mistakes:
1. Not specifying H-bond dimers for high BP
2. Saying alkaline hydrolysis is reversible
3. Writing HCl as product with NH₃ (it's NH₄Cl)
4. Poor mechanism drawing (missing charges/arrows)
5. Not knowing reactivity order
6. Confusing acid/alkaline hydrolysis products
